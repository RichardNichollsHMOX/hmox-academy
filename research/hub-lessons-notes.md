## 1. Welcome to Hub Lite
- One-line intro; names the four core ideas: Tier Methodology, Five Lenses, Tier Slippage, Leverage Trap.

## 2. Buy It Right (companion guide to a free 90-minute live session, Richard Nicholls)
- **Tier Methodology**: reads any HMO on three things, **amenity, size and condition**, across five tiers. House rule: "A brand-new conversion is usually a Tier 2 asset wearing a Tier 1 price. Read the tier, not the finish."
- **Leverage trap**: a new HMO trades at roughly **110–120%** of its long-term rent for about **12–18 months**; gearing to that premium means the 3–5 year refinance falls short. "Don't finance off the new-build premium. Finance on a blended rent roll — what three-to-five-year-old HMOs actually achieve."
- **Tier slippage**: unmaintained, it slips a tier "roughly every twelve to eighteen months" (Tier module says 24–36).
- **Holding the home tier**: compliance current, condition maintained, house harmony and demand, management standard. "The tier is earned every year, not banked once."
- **Onboarding**: about **10 days** for a very good self-managed HMO; most take **3–6 weeks**. PAT, building-regs sign-off, Legionella, asbestos and buildings insurance are now asked for on almost every sale.
- "Not knowing something's missing is the real risk. Knowing it's missing — and pricing it in, or putting it right between exchange and completion — is just diligence."
- **Valuer's lens**: an operating investment, not a house. Five things carry value above the bricks: secured lawful use; specification and amenity; a deep lettings market; evidenced income ("asserted, not evidenced, gets down-valued"); clean management. "Justify every single penny above bricks-and-mortar value."
- An HMO must beat the average local buy-to-let (BTL 2–3% net vs HMO 7–9%).
- **30-second sense check**: gross annual income ÷ rooms ÷ 12 = average monthly rent per room; compare with the area.
- Claims: no down valuations "this year"; 17+ years' experience; "well over 100 live HMOs assessed every month". House rule: "Data, not opinion".
- **PIR** (Property Information Report): 16 sections, "a brief, not a brochure"; numbers trace to the onboarding bundle; discrepancies flagged, not reconciled; optimisation shown as upside, never in the headline. **Buyer Valuation Pack** goes with the mortgage application for the lender's surveyor. **Handover Report** on completion.
- **By tier** (value basis moves from income at the top to bricks and mortar at the bottom). "Higher rents alone don't refinance. A lender pays for a scheme — planning, space, amenity — not a repaint."
  - T1 turnkey: valued on yield; beware the **stacked premium** (above-area rent and a tighter yield).
  - T2: "Buying after the dip means the vendor funded the slippage — not you." Valued on yield.
  - T3: about **60%** of what HMO X sold last month; budget a room refresh as long-standing tenants leave.
  - T4: buy at or near bricks and mortar; a rent rise alone won't give a commercial refinance; you need a genuine scheme (planning class, space, amenity, en-suites).
  - T5: "Get the tenants out, then do the work." Never buy a non-compliant HMO with tenants in. Protect **continuous use**: an empty HMO can be refused planning.
- **Source to Order**: "organised matchmaking", not sourcing; £49 + VAT a month while searching; free Hub year on completion.

## 3. Three HMO deals. Which would you buy? (companion guide)
- **Six things before strategy**: location, tenant type, budget, your plan, management, scale of operation. "Every decision here is tenant-led."
- Location: demand = rooms wanted, not rooms listed; **when** Article 4 arrived matters (recent = possible pre-deadline glut). Higher gross yield = market pricing in weaker fundamentals.
- Professional, student and social/supported tenants are "three different businesses".
- **Two valuation bases**: bricks and mortar (comparable house sales, capital growth) vs commercial/investment (income-based, bigger debt, higher refinance bar). A portfolio blend is often sensible.
- **Route 1, develop new**: premium rent and cash recycling, but too much new stock in many areas, build costs (~£200k for a 6-bed all-en-suite outside London/higher-value South East), Article 4 delays, less control over end GDV.
- **Route 2, add value** (T4/T5, at or below bricks and mortar): retiring-landlord stock with established use. Watch inherited compliance and why the seller is selling. "A lick of paint and a change of tenant type will not move a valuation."
- **Route 3, buy established**: often cheaper to buy than build; easier due diligence; income day one. Watch compliance, overpaying, capital tied up.
- "Be led by the deal, not loyalty to a strategy. This is not choosing a football team."
- Legal note in text: C3 to C4 under **Class L permitted development** covers up to six persons; seven or more is Sui Generis and needs planning.

## 4. No speaker. Just you. (open Q&A companion guide)
- **Buyer Demand analyser** preview: tests a deal against five buyer types and recommends a sales route; residential exits sit under **XHMO** (selling former HMOs to family buyers).
- Market: local beats national; stagnating rents usually mean oversupply. Prices typically agreed **3–4% below asking**. HMO X discourages "testing the market" (unsettles tenants; a failed sale harms later refinance or sale).
- **Article 4**: the run-up (especially after the **12-month notice**) often brings a development boom; oversupply can take 2–4 years to settle; values often dipped rather than rose. Long term good for sustainability. Wales takes a national approach. Immediate directions do the real damage.
- **Vacant possession after RRA** (Alice Ibbotson, Property Shield): Section 21 gone; need a Section 8 ground; selling ground notice is **4 months** (was 2). Rule: sale ground can't be used in the first **12 months** of a tenancy and property can't be relet for **12 months** after possession. Family-member ground misuse carries a penalty.
- **Ground 4A** (student HMOs): needs a specific written statement, not just listing the ground; possession date must fall **1 June–30 September**; 4-month notice; joint-tenancy early leavers are an unresolved problem.
- **Rates** (George Sanford, Vibe Finance): plan around roughly **5.5–6.5%**, not the 2–3% pre-2022. "If a rate of around 6% to 6.5% makes a deal fall apart, the deal probably wasn't good enough in the first place." Non-compliance can mean valuation as a single dwelling, retentions or decline.
- **Net yield benchmark**: long-standing **8–10% net**. Running costs ~18% of rent at £1,000/room but close to half at £450/room, so a 17% gross in the North East may be only 8–9% net.
- **Fire doors** (Alice Ibbotson, Property Shield): the FRA should comment on them; timber doors to **BS 8214** (a standard, not law), usually **44mm**, intumescent strips, smoke seals, self-closing; gaps outside **2–4mm** are a red flag. Survey by an accredited specialist, not an installer. Budget specialist fitting from day one (~£3,000–£4,000 on a £200k+ conversion).
- Rent evidence: always ask; expect at least **12–18 months** of evidence (statements), not a spreadsheet.
- **Licences are not transferable**: buying in own name means a new fit and proper test; buying the company is less clear-cut (ask council).
- Agents: look for a "hybrid agent". "At 6% to 8% you'll get a 6% to 8% service." Check exit clauses.
- Buying cheap isn't value-add: "The surveyor will say you were the market."
- When to sell: when fully compliant. Gaps: offers around **80p in the pound**; fully prepared: **92–95p**.

## 5. HMO X Tier Methodology (Richard Nicholls)
- Refinance question: "which tier will this asset occupy across the fixed period — and at what percentage of its sustainable home tier rent?"
- Evidence base: **over 150 HMO inspections a month**, own refinance/sold dataset, HMO auction house (launched **June 2025**).
- **Three anchors**: **home tier** (tier the fundamentals sustainably command; the analytical 100%); **tier slippage**; **leverage trap**.
- **Five lenses**: sustainable-leveraging (developers), valuation-clarity (buyers), optimisation (operators), realisation (sellers), value-add (rejuvenators). Most investors use 3–4 over a portfolio's life.
- Bands are "never by price alone"; hierarchical in quality, not opportunity.
- **Critical break T4/T5**: T4 is an operating tier; T5 an opportunity tier (value in rights and footprint).
- New HMO can trade one tier above home tier for **12–18 months**; poorly run can drift one below.
- **Four criteria for true T1**: all four or it is T2 (criteria named in Buying your first HMO: room size, location, acoustics and privacy, functionality; no thresholds given in this text). "The Honest Test": fail one and launch rent is ~115% of home tier.
- **Tier slippage**: one tier every **24–36 months** without capex and management; T1 becomes T2 by year three; ~115% at launch to ~84% by year five.
- **Derby worked example**: 6-bed, launch £4,310 pcm / £51,720 a year; home tier T2 at ~£45,000. Causes of slide: premium fades, competition, weak management, no capex reserve for the **£500–£1,000 per room per year** refresh and year-five refit. Actively managed: rental inflation 2–4% on quality stock; at 12% yield Year 5 value **£400,000 vs £316,000** under slippage (£84,000 gap).
- **Leverage discipline rule**: underwrite term debt on the blended rent roll across the fix; working blend Year 1 115%, Year 2 104%, Years 3–5 100%; stay at or below 100% of home tier on average.
- **Affordability floor**: 30% of gross income benchmark; ONS ASHE median full-time earnings is a primary input. Midlands/northern HMO earners £25k–£35k sit at T2–T3; safe assumption: T2 is the sustainable income tier there.

## 6. Buying your first HMO
- Recommended entry point: "the quality established HMO — a Tier 2 or Tier 3 asset". "Development is for the experienced, not the keen."
- First-time buyers lose money "on the wrong assumptions": asking yield real, pack complete, lender funding Year 1 rents in Year 5.
- Budget has seven lines (table not in text); the seventh, post-completion working capital, is most underestimated (first mortgage interest, licence fee, void cover, deposit protection, buffer).
- "Pick the corridor before you pick the asset." Want two distinct demand pillars within 20 minutes.
- Article 4 constrains new supply and protects existing ("grandfather right").
- Natural market: the **2018–2024 resale cohort** of quality established HMOs.
- Finance: HMO-specialist broker from day one; personal vs SPV settled with an accountant before offer; five-year fix is the default; agreement in principle before the offer.
- Offer walk-back: blended rent roll (95–100% of current achieved gross for an established T2) → apply ICR (**145%**) at stress rate (**7–8%**) → max loan → ÷ LTV (**75%**) → max price. Asking, clearing (auction) and sold yields are "never averaged". Applied yields: **11.5–12.5%** Midlands, **8.5–10%** southern professional.

## 7. Compliance & Legislation (Alice Ibbotson)
- HMO compliance is "a separate discipline": **eleven regimes, not five**; four features: scale, inspection cadence, local administration, enforcement consequence.
- **Licence conditions** (10–40 pages): occupancy, room size (**6.51 m²** single, **10.22 m²** double), amenities (typically **1 bathroom and 1 WC per 5 persons**), named manager (change = variation), condition, tenant management. Most common failure: "operational drift".
- **Management of HMOs (England) Regulations 2006** apply to every HMO, licensed or not; each breach is a separate offence. Councils now issue a separate Civil Penalty Notice (up to £30,000) per breach, and some describe the income as funding their teams.
- **Fire**: Fire Safety Order 2005; FRA by a competent person, reviewed annually; Grade A or D2 interlinked alarm (BS 5839-6), tested weekly; FD30 doors with seals and closers; emergency lighting (BS 5266-1) where escape is compromised; CO alarms. "There is no compliance position above the fire safety position."
- **EICR** every 5 years; C1, C2 and FI items closed within 28 days. Gas (CP12) annually; commonest failure is not serving the copy on tenants.
- **RRA 2025**: ASTs replaced by periodic assured tenancies; Section 21 abolished; rent increases once in 12 months, challengeable at tribunal. Right to Rent, Tenant Fees Act and prescribed documents unchanged.
- **Deposits**: protect within **30 days** in DPS, TDS or MyDeposits and serve Prescribed Information; cap 5 weeks' rent (6 at £50k+). Penalty 1–3x deposit and possession blocked. Common failures: PI not served, wrong scheme's form, scheme moved without re-serving.
- **EPC**: minimum E now; C for new tenancies from 2028 and all from 2030 (policy, cost cap); route to C for typical 6-bed £3,000–£8,000. "EPC trajectory is not an environmental conversation. It is a tier-defence conversation."
- **HHSRS**: 29 hazards; Category 1 (council must act) vs Category 2 (may act).
- Three external moments: licence renewal, refinance, resale (scrambled pack typically **5–10%** below). Enforcement: CPN up to £30,000, RRO up to **12 months**, banning orders, prosecution.

## 8. Planning & Licensing (Carianne Wells)
- Licence depends on planning, but planning is not implied by a licence.
- Use classes (1987 Order): **C3** household; **C4** 3–6 unrelated, 2+ households sharing; **Sui Generis** 7+. "The seventh occupant is the universal stop": no PD route to Sui Generis.
- GPDO 2015: C3 to C4 PD right since 2010; removed by Article 4. Extensions need their own consent.
- Article 4 list "at May 2026" (moves; check council). Concentration caps typically **10–20%**. Dual effect: constraint and protection.
- **Established use**: time limits in TCPA 1990, amended by LURA 2023 from April 2024; year-count depends on facts (no figure given). Evidence: planning history, council tax, electoral roll, tenancies, licensing history, statutory declarations.
- **LDC**: CLEUD (existing) vs CLOPUD (proposed); fee £103 to several hundred; statutory 8 weeks; 8–12 weeks end to end. Failure patterns: thin evidence, break in use, wrong use claimed. Rules: "Every HMO inside an Article 4 corridor requires one"; HMO X funds the LDC on bespoke sales listings where needed.
- Licensing: Mandatory (5+, 2+ households), Additional, Selective. Clean application 8–16 weeks; renewal judged on standards current at renewal.
- Incomplete-pack yield discount ~0.75 points ≈ £20k–£30k on a £40k-rent 6-bed.

## 9. Financing & Leveraging (George Sanford, Vibe Finance)
- 8–10 active HMO lenders (May 2026); appetite is a moving target. Large HMOs (7+, Sui Generis) default to investment valuation.
- Term mortgage 75% LTV (some 80%). "Bridging and refurb-to-let are tools, not strategies."
- ICR **125% SPV / 145% higher-rate personal**, stress ~**5.5%** on five-year fixes. Valuation basis is "the first underwriting question, not the last".
- SPV is the default for scaling investors (Section 24); accountant first, then broker.
- Blend here: Year 1 at 90% T1 income, Year 2 75/25 T1/T2, Years 3–5 full T2. Derby at 11%: £470k vs £345k.

## 10. Working with a Managing Agent (with Jen Dunwell, Arena Property)
- "The agent runs the asset. The owner runs the agent." Penalties are served on the licence holder.
- RRA as stated: APTs from **1 May 2026**; Section 8 on Form 3A; rent increase Section 13 on Form 4A, once per 12 months, 2 months' notice; civil penalties **£7,000 per breach / £40,000 per offence**; RRO **24 months**, extends to freeholders behind rent-to-rent; Information Sheet by **31 May 2026**; PRS Database late 2026, Ombudsman 2028.
- Fees **8–15%** of gross (8–10 basic, 10–12 full, 12–15 premium); certificates and council licence fees (£600–£1,500) usually extra. "The right agent at 12 % is invariably cheaper than the wrong agent at 9 %."

## Quiz candidates
1. HMO X's house line on a brand-new conversion? **"Usually a Tier 2 asset wearing a Tier 1 price"**. Wrong: always Tier 1; Tier 3 until let; valued on bricks and mortar only. Why: read the tier, not the finish.
2. A new HMO typically trades at what share of its long-term rent? **110–120%**. Wrong: 90–95%; 100%; 150–160%. Why: newness premium.
3. What should term debt be underwritten against? **Blended rent roll across the fixed period**. Wrong: launch rent; asking yield; highest comparable. Why: leverage discipline rule.
4. The 30-second sense check? **Gross annual income ÷ rooms ÷ 12**. Wrong: price ÷ rooms; net ÷ 12; gross ÷ price. Why: average room rent vs area.
5. What lifts a refinance value on a T4? **A genuine scheme: planning change, space, amenity or en-suites**. Wrong: repaint and raise rents; tenant change; bought below asking. Why: "you were the market".
6. Why buy a T5 vacant? **Tenanted non-compliant HMO is a liability**. Wrong: vacant is cheaper to insure; lenders demand it for all HMOs; licence transfers. Why: fines outweigh income.
7. Selling ground notice under RRA? **Four months; not in first 12 months**. Wrong: two months any time; Section 21; six weeks. Why: Section 8 sale ground.
8. Use class for 7+ unrelated sharers? **Sui Generis, always needs planning**. Wrong: C4; C3; C4 unless Article 4. Why: the seventh occupant stop.
9. Does an HMO licence transfer on sale? **No; new licence and fit and proper test**. Wrong: transfers automatically; only mandatory ones; only with agent. Why: licences aren't transferable.
10. Deposit protection window? **30 days, with Prescribed Information**. Wrong: 14 days; 60 days; at tenancy end. Why: Housing Act 2004 Part 6.
11. Fire door gap red flag? **Outside 2–4mm**. Wrong: under 10mm OK; any visible gap; 6–8mm normal. Why: BS 8214 practice.
12. Gas and EICR cadence? **Gas yearly, EICR every 5 years**. Wrong: both yearly; gas 5-yearly; EICR 10-yearly. Why: 1998 and 2020 Regs.
13. Statutory minimum single room? **6.51 m²**. Wrong: 4.64; 8.00; 10.22. Why: 10.22 is a double.
14. HMO X long-standing net yield benchmark? **8–10% net**. Wrong: 2–3%; 15–17%; 5–6%. Why: headroom over ~6.5% lending.
15. Fully prepared HMO price vs one with gaps? **92–95p vs about 80p in the pound**. Wrong: same; 100p vs 95p; 70p vs 60p. Why: compliance is part of the price.

## 1. Self-Management (HMO X core team; "Article 6" of the series)

- HMO X's entry point is "the quality established HMO": a Tier 2 or Tier 3 asset, fully compliant, professionally managed or expertly self-managed. New conversions and Tier 5 rejuvenation are "the next chapter, taken once the operator has done the operating".
- **Tier slippage:** "Without active capex and disciplined management, HMO stock decays roughly one tier every 24 to 36 months."
- There are two good routes: self-management or an excellent managing agent. HMO X supports both, and the choice belongs to the operator.
- **Four conditions** for self-management: time, temperament, local knowledge and standards. If all four are present, self-manage. If any one is missing, use an agent "from day one".
- House line (HMO X Operator Rule): "Self-management is a vocational discipline, not a cost-saving exercise." The self-manager who chose it to save the agent fee "will be priced for that decision at every refinance, every relicence and every resale."
- Responsibilities sit with the legal owner whatever the operating model. "The managing agent is the operator's hands; the operator is still the head and the conscience."
- **Six working territories**, to the standard of a good agent:
  - Compliance hygiene: every certificate in date, with renewals scheduled ahead. The pack should be "readable in five minutes". "Compliance is not paperwork — it is operations."
  - Tenant communication: enquiries answered within a working day, and maintenance triaged the same day.
  - Asset condition: communal areas kept to T2 standard all year. The state of the communal areas is the strongest signal a tenant reads.
  - Documentation: deposits protected within 30 days with Prescribed Information served. Right to Rent checked before the tenancy starts. Inventories kept for every room.
  - Financial reporting: monthly asset-level accounts and a room-by-room rent roll, with voids tracked.
  - Continuous improvement: an annual review against the operating plan.
- **Lettings:** SpareRoom is "the workhorse" for room lets, OpenRent for whole-property lets, and local Facebook or university groups in student areas. A viewing is "a mutual interview". Referencing through a provider costs £20 to £40 per applicant; the operator takes up the previous-landlord reference personally.
- **Onboarding:** done in the first 48 hours after an offer is accepted. Serve the How to Rent guide, EPC, gas safety record and licence at or before the tenancy starts. (The How to Rent guide is pre-RRA wording.)
- **Retention:** a renewal rate of 40 to 50% per room per year is achievable on a well-managed T2. Below 35 to 40% is a warning sign.
- **Contractors:** Gas Safe engineer (annual CP12) and a NICEIC or NAPIT electrician (5-yearly EICR, with C1 and C2 remedials closed within 28 days). The handyman is "the operator's single most useful contractor".
- **Triage order:**
  1. Awaab's Law hazards (damp, mould, structural, electrical or heating failure): statutory timescales.
  2. Safety issues: response the same day and a contractor within 24 hours.
  3. Amenity issues: triaged within the working day, fixed within the week.
  4. Cosmetic issues: batched for the next visit.
- **Capex** has two streams: a small monthly accrual, and a major cycle every 5 to 7 years.
- **Systems:**
  - Accounts are kept per asset, never blended across the portfolio.
  - Software (Arthur, PayProp, Hammock) usually starts to pay between the second and third HMO.
  - Set certificate reminders at 90, 60 and 30 days before expiry.
  - Use one bank account per HMO or per company.
  - HMO tax is specialist-accountant territory.
- **Five failure modes**, each with its own check: tier drift ("what tier would a buyer price it at today?"), compliance drift (can I produce the full pack in five minutes?), tenant relationship drift (the renewal rate), voids drift, and operator burnout. "Failure modes are not failures of the operator. They are signals to act."
- Under the RRA, a deposit failure now blocks possession entirely, and a Decent Homes breach is an ombudsman matter.
- Prices quoted:
  - Health Check Report: £995 + VAT, 10% off for Hub members.
  - Hub: £499 + VAT a year.

## 2. Growing your Portfolio

- House line: "A portfolio is a strategy, not a number." The HMO X Portfolio Principle is that "Adding another HMO is not the same as building a portfolio."
- Four complementary choices:
  - **Strategy:** anchor the portfolio at T2 (around 10 sqm en-suite rooms, 6 to 8 beds).
  - **Location:** geographic concentration helps; two HMOs within 20 miles share contractors and agents. "Diversify geographically" in practice often means "operate inefficiently across multiple markets."
  - **Style:** keep the tenant type consistent.
  - **Exit shape:** build a portfolio one buyer can underwrite as one deal.
- HMO X Scaling Rule: "Pick the corridor before you pick the second asset. Pick the tier before you pick the corridor. Pick the exit shape before you pick the tier."
- **Lending constraints from deal three onwards:**
  - Lender concentration caps, which are rarely published.
  - Portfolio stress tests, often from 4 properties or £1m of debt.
  - Refinance staggering: space acquisitions 9 to 12 months apart.
- George Sanford Stacking Rule: "The first HMO mortgage decides the relationship. The third decides the portfolio."
- **Leverage discipline:** underwrite term debt against the blended home tier % over the fixed period, not the launch peak. A conversion at 115% in year 1 that settles to 100% by year 3 should be underwritten at a blend of "typically 100% to 105% of home tier".
- **Home tier %:** a new conversion sits at 115%+ for the first 12 to 18 months. A passively held asset drifts below 85% by year five. Example slide: 100% to 92% to 86% to below 85%.
- **Rolling annual tier audit:** one line per asset showing home tier, current %, prior-year %, the change, and the action.
- **Capex:**
  - £500 to £1,000 per room per year, accrued monthly.
  - A £15,000 to £30,000 refit every 5 to 7 years.
  - For 8 assets of 6 rooms each, the rolling pot comes to £24,000 to £48,000 a year.
- **Agents:** the "single-relationship principle" is one agent, or two in neighbouring areas. "The right agent at 12% management fee is invariably cheaper across the cycle than the wrong agent at 9%."
- **Compliance uniformity:**
  - Alice Ibbotson's "eleven regulated regimes": gas, EICR, FRA, EPC, fire alarm and emergency lighting, PAT where required, deposit protection, RRA-compliant tenancies, Right to Rent, HHSRS and Legionella.
  - A portfolio is judged by its worst-managed asset.
- **Single points of failure audit:** one agent, one lender, one area, one key contractor, one refinance window, one operator. Name each one, price it and put a fallback in place.
- **Pen-portrait of a mid-sized portfolio:**
  - 6 to 8 T2s in one Article 4 corridor, each at 95 to 105% of home tier.
  - An agent on a 10 to 12% fee, with two lenders.
  - Held in an SPV, so a share sale is possible at year 10.
  - Rent roll of £280k to £360k.
  - Net yield of 8 to 11% before leverage.
  - EPC C on every asset.
- **Development** is "for the experienced, not the keen". There are two routes:
  - T1 builds: 15 sqm+ rooms with kitchenettes, 8 to 12 beds, commercial development finance.
  - T4/T5 rejuvenation: Article 4 grandfather rights cannot be replicated, and rejuvenation costs 40 to 60% of new-build cost per room.
- Have an HMO solicitor read the auction legal pack before bidding: "the gavel is the exchange".
- The Developing HMOs course goes live in Autumn 2026, for Hub members only.
- **Exit products:**
  - Exit Planning Report: for sellers 12 to 36 months from sale, with three scenarios.
  - Bespoke Sales Appraisal: for sellers within 12 months.

  - The module numbering differs between articles. Compliance is Module 3 in one article and Module 4 in another; Finance is Module 2 in one and 05 in another.
  - The weekly live is called "Tuesday live at 7pm" in some places and "Tuesday Bitesize" in others.

## 3. Exit Planning and Selling (Richard Nicholls, with Georgia Hopewell, George Sanford, Carianne Wells and Alice Ibbotson)

- "Every HMO ends in a sale." The seller controls four things: who controls the sale, the timeline, the price and the tax treatment.
- HMO X Seller Rule: "Sale-readiness is a daily operating discipline, not a one-off project." Exit planning starts on day one of ownership.
- **Four disciplines:**
  - Tier defence: the same £500 to £1,000 per room per year and £15k to £30k every 5 to 7 years. "A new Tier 1 conversion is a Tier 2 asset by year three."
  - Compliance hygiene.
  - Planning evidence: inside Article 4, "the LDC at sale is rarely optional".
  - Documentation discipline.
- **Five operator lenses:** acquisition, operating, optimisation, realisation (the fourth) and strategic.
- **Six reasons to sell:**
  1. Strategic fit has shifted.
  2. The economic case favours sale (but often the answer is refinance).
  3. Tier slippage is uneconomic to reverse (example: £40k of work for an uplift that only supports £20k).
  4. Planning or regulation has changed.
  5. Life circumstances.
  6. An unusual market premium.
- "Hold is a legitimate answer."
- The market is two-tier. One well-run asset sold at 10.42% gross yield in a market around 11%.
- **Seven seller types:** retiring operator, flipper, HMO-to-HMO reinvestor, urgent seller, optimising seller, seller who needs support, asset-class exiter.
- **Tier bands at sale:**
  - T2 benchmark yields: 11.5 to 12.5% gross in the Midlands and North, 9.5 to 10.75% in the South.

  - T5: the auction is the natural channel.

## 5. The HMO X Glossary (key exact definitions)

**Tiers**
- **T1 Premium / Studio:** "15 sqm+ rooms, A1 city-centre or hospital-corridor location, hotel-grade fit-out, 8–12+ beds run as a branded co-living scheme. A discrete product class — not a destination any 6-bed terrace can climb to."
- **T2 New Quality:** "Recently refurbished, full en-suite ~10 sqm, modern finish, professionally managed, 6–8 beds. The defensible mainstream."
- **T3 Standard / Older En-Suite:** "T2 stock at the end of its first fixed cycle, or spacious non-en-suite. Good order, visibly ageing, 4–7 beds."
- **T4 Basic / Functional:** "Older non-en-suite stock in secondary locations, dated but trading and compliant, 2–5 beds, often a live-in private landlord."
- **T5 Distressed / Redevelopment:** "Substantially degraded, compliance-marginal, often part-vacant — but holding grandfather rights or planning consents that cannot be replicated." Thesis: value-add.

**Tier Methodology terms**
- **Home tier %:** "115%+ = launch premium; 100% = settled; 85–95% = drift; <85% = subtier."
- **Tier slippage and tier defence:** slippage is roughly one tier every 24 to 36 months. Defence is a £500 to £1,000 per room annual refresh and a major refit every 5 to 7 years.
- **Leverage trap and discipline rule:** "leveraging against peak Year-1 rent (115% of home tier) on an asset that will settle to 100% within 24–36 months". The rule is to underwrite against the blended rent roll, "at or below 100% of home tier on average".

**Planning**
- **C3:** a single household.
- **C4:** "3 to 6 unrelated occupants forming more than one household".
- **Sui generis HMO:** 7 or more people. "no permitted-development route into Sui Generis, anywhere in England. The seventh occupant is the universal stop."
- **GPDO:** pre-grants permission for C3 to C4 outside Article 4 areas.
- **Article 4 Direction:** removes the C3 to C4 right, so a new small HMO in the zone needs full planning permission.
- **Grandfather rights:** a lawful use that pre-dates the rule change. The right passes to every later owner and is "irreplaceable supply".
- **LDC:** issued by the council. Typical timeline "8–12 weeks".
- **HMO Concentration Cap:** typically 10 to 20% of dwellings within a defined radius.

**Licensing and compliance**
- **Housing Act 2004:** Parts 1, 2 and 6.
- **Mandatory licensing:** 5+ unrelated people in 2+ households. Five-year cycle; fees £600 to £1,500+.
- **Additional licensing:** typically C4 HMOs with 3 to 4 occupants.
- **Selective licensing:** all privately rented homes in an area; "the broadest and fastest-expanding regime".
- **HHSRS:** 29 hazard categories. Category 1 means mandatory enforcement; Category 2 is discretionary.
- **CP12:** annual. Given to existing tenants within 28 days.
- **EICR:** every 5 years. C1, C2 and FI findings must be closed within 28 days.
- **FRA:** under the Fire Safety Order 2005. Reviewed annually, after a material change, and after any incident.
- **EPC:** minimum E today. C is proposed for new tenancies from 2028 and all tenancies from 2030.
- **Deposits:** protected with DPS, TDS or MyDeposits within 30 days, with Prescribed Information. Capped at 5 weeks' rent.
- **Right to Rent:** Immigration Act 2014.

**Tenancy after the RRA**
- **RRA:** the Renters' Rights Act 2025, in force across 2026.
- **APT:** "rolling monthly, no fixed term, no break clause."
- **Section 8:** served on Form 3A, on evidenced grounds.
- **PRS Ombudsman:** "Free, fast, documentary — sits below the First-tier Tribunal."
- **Landlord Database:** visible to tenants, councils, lenders and future buyers.
- **Decent Homes Standard:** being phased in across 2026 to 2027.

**Finance**
- **LTV:** measured against the lender's valuation, not the price. Standard 75%, sometimes 80%.
- **Applied yield:** 11.5 to 12.5% gross across most of the Midlands and North in 2026.
- **ICR:** stress rate 7 to 8% in 2026. 125% for SPV or basic-rate applicants; 145% for higher-rate personal applicants.
- **Bricks-and-mortar valuation:** the default for most small C4 HMOs.
- **Investment valuation:** the default for sui generis; available on well-specified C4s.
- **SPV:** SIC code 68209.
- **Section 24:** Finance (No. 2) Act 2015.
- **Bridging and refurb-to-let:** "Tools to reach refinance, not strategies."

**Sale and products**
- **Asking / Clearing / Sold** (glossary version): asking is what disciplined sellers present in the Committed Sale market; clearing is "what the actual auction transaction delivered on the day"; sold is historical comparables over the preceding 12 to 36 months. "Never average the three."
- **Hub Lite:** free; includes "the Tuesday Bitesize".
- **The Hub:** £499 a year.
- **Type A Gifted-Access Year:** "Twelve months of Hub access gifted to every buyer who completes on an HMO X listing."
- **AI Compliance Scan:** checks the pack against all eleven regulated regimes.
- **HMO X Auctions:** "the UK's first specialist HMO auction house, launched June 2025."

## 7. RRA Article 1: The RRA Tier Split

- The legal baseline is the GOV.UK landlord guidance of 1 May 2026.
- House line: "The market was already splitting. The RRA is the accelerant."
- **Three readers, one pack:**
  - The buyer reads for inheritable risk.
  - The surveyor reads whether the valuation can be defended (a tribunal challenge to the rent roll).
  - The council reads for enforcement exposure: rent repayment orders now reach two years' rent, and "we may" has become "we will take action". That last point is a field observation, not law.
- RRA Valuation Rule: "the documented HMO commands the premium and the undocumented HMO carries the discount… Process is the asset."
- **Worked example:** a 6-bed listed at £315k against a T3 comparable of £375k. The £60k discount "evaporates" once you find the failings:
  - Information Sheet not served.
  - Prescribed Information not re-served after the agent changed.
  - A rent rise made by email without Section 13.
  - A fire-door requirement at the next licence renewal.
- House line: "a bad HMO, you cannot buy it cheaply enough."
- RRA Acquisition Rule: "A discount to comparable is not a discount until the inheritable RRA position is costed."
- A true T5 is underwritten on its footprint and rights, not on its rent roll.
- **Rent-to-rent:** the freeholder inherits the tenants, deposits, notices and licence liability, and can face the rent repayment order. "rent-to-rent has become more risky under the RRA, not less."
- **The tiers after the RRA:** the five bands hold, but the operating standard needed to hold each tier rises.
  - T4 is the most exposed; it can become a T5 "in valuation terms long before it becomes one in physical terms".
  - T5 changes least.
- Also states: T1 becomes T2 within 3 years, and T3 within 5, without capex.

## 8. RRA Article 2: From Tenancy to Operating Spine

- Rachel Lewis: "the RRA is a process."
- RRA Operating Rule: "the document is the position… Process beats panic, and paperwork beats memory."
- **What changed in law:**
  - ASTs rolled into APTs with no fixed end date.
  - Section 13 rent increases need Form 4A, at least two months' notice, no more than once a year, and can be challenged at the First-tier Tribunal if above open market rent.
  - Information Sheet by 31 May 2026 for written tenancies; written key terms for verbal ones.
  - No rent requested before signing, and no bidding.
  - Pet requests must be considered; a refusal needs a valid reason.
  - Deposits are "a condition precedent to possession".
- **Five actions to evidence by 31 May 2026:**
  1. The Information Sheet served and recorded for every written tenancy.
  2. Written key terms served for every verbal tenancy.
  3. Written confirmation from every agent of what they have done.
  4. Every new tenancy since 1 May checked: APT, not an old AST template.
  5. No rent taken before signing.
- Service is room by room: 12 six-bed HMOs means 72 service events.
- **Three failure modes:** served but not recorded; delegated but not checked ("the buck stops with the landlord"); assuming the Information Sheet was the only action.
- RRA Evidence Rule: three documents per room. These are the deposit pack, the Section 13 file (comparables stored in case of a tribunal) and the notice trail.
- **Possession:**
  - Section 8 notice periods are often 4 months; anti-social behaviour is faster.
  - Rent arrears of 3 months is the level at which the court must grant possession; below that it has discretion.
  - Sale and owner-occupation grounds are limited in the first 12 months of a tenancy.
  - A commercial settlement is a case-by-case option (Neil Baldock).
- **Sharper enforcement focus:** licensing, fire safety and HHSRS, and management regulations.
- **Operating habits:**
  - Robust referencing.
  - "Do not lower quality standards in tenant selection to fill voids."
  - Bring back the house meeting.
  - Log everything.
- Alice: "most enforcement breach is administrative, not malicious."

## 9. RRA Article 3: The End of the Hands-Off HMO

- "The HMO was never genuinely hands-off. It was, in the best operations, well-delegated."
- RRA Oversight Rule: the landlord "cannot delegate the awareness. The buck stops with the landlord."
- If an agent has not been in touch about the RRA, "alarm bells should be ringing".
- **Questions to put to your agent in writing:**
  - What has been done, served and evidenced.
  - The Information Sheet position for each room.
  - Section 13 comparables.
  - Deposits and Prescribed Information.
  - The anti-social behaviour log.
  - The licensing calendar.
- **The buck-stops standard:**
  - Know the portfolio's status.
  - Get a monthly written agent report.
  - Run an annual review of each asset.
  - Keep named contacts.
- **Pseudo-passive positions:**
  - Has not visited in 18 months.
  - Cannot name the tenants.
  - Signed the licence renewal without seeing the property.
- **Rent-to-rent safeguards:**
  - An audit right in the lease.
  - Monthly reporting.
  - An annual compliance review.
  - Clear terms for the end of the lease.
- **Cadence:**
  - Weekly: the rent ledger and the maintenance log.
  - Monthly: the agent report and the Compliance Briefing.
  - Quarterly: a property visit or inspection report.
  - Annually: a full review, including the tier read, a room-by-room compliance audit, a rent benchmark, capex, the licence calendar, and the decision to hold, exit or scale.
- RRA Active-Landlord Rule: "the landlord runs the asset or the asset runs the landlord. Delegation is permitted. Detachment is exposed." House line: "Run the HMO. Don't let the HMO run you."

## 1. Developer lens
**Purpose**: the "sustainable-leveraging lens" for creating an HMO and financing it so it survives the first refinance. **Rule**: "Leverage to the tier it holds, not the tier it performs." Every development decision is a leverage decision. Day-one rent sets the valuation, the valuation sets the debt, and the debt decides whether the scheme survives.

**Framework**
- Treat launch rent as a premium, not the baseline. Write it as a % of home tier.
- Derby six-bed reference example (12% yield): geared at 115% and reverting to 100% leaves an equity gap of about **£93,000**. Sliding from a Tier 1 launch to a Tier 3 reality (84%) leaves about **£196,000**. Running at 100% instead of 84% is worth about **£84,000** at year five.
- **Cost-led GDV error**: pushing the GDV up until the appraisal balances. A valuer prices what the HMO earns, not what it cost.
- **Gear to the blend**: Year 1 115%, Year 2 104%, Years 3 to 5 100%. The average across the fix should be **at or below 100%**. Bank the premium as a bonus, but never borrow against it.
- "Many HMO developers forget to become landlords."
- Five yes/no questions before drawing debt: have I fixed the home tier? Does it meet all four Tier 1 criteria today? Have I counted demand against new supply? Is the GDV backed by rent, not cost? Am I gearing to the blend?

**Diagnostic: Is it really a Tier 1?** Tier 1 is "a discrete product class, not a finish level". All four criteria must be met today. Fail one and it is "a Tier 2 with a good finish".
1. Room size: **14 to 16 sqm single, 18 to 22 sqm dual**. The usual 8 to 10 sqm en-suite room does not qualify.
2. Location: **A1 only** (walk to the city-centre station and amenities, or on a hospital corridor).
3. Acoustics: hotel standard, costing **£1,500 to £3,000 a room** above a normal refurb.
4. Functionality: proper food prep, storage, a desk and work-from-home space, **1Gb+ broadband**.
- A Tier 1 needs a refresh of about **£500 to £1,000 a room a year**, or it drifts to Tier 2 within **24 to 36 months**.
- Count demand as well as supply. At **85% occupancy, Tier 1 net yield falls below Tier 2**.
- If it fails a criterion, underwrite at Tier 2 from Year 1.

**Worked Scheme: what a GDV appraisal does** (method drawn from several anonymised schemes)
- It serves one number: the refinance figure a specialist lender's panel valuer will support about 12 months after completion.
- Build the rent room by room from let evidence, not asking rents. Then **blend it across the fix and round down** (for example £53,500 adopted at £50,000).
- Build the yield as a band from a set of factors: Article 4, lot size, location, specification, finance sentiment, EPC trajectory and depth of evidence. Say in writing why a tighter yield was turned down.
- Use **two bases**. The sales basis is usually **50 to 100 basis points wider** than the refinance basis, because of stamp duty, caution and no stabilised track record.
- Cross-check three ways: bricks-and-mortar £ per sq m, a net-yield stress test and £ per bed.
- A refinance is won with **12 to 18 months of documented trading**. Bring in a broker before the build. The appraisal's real job is to name the gap before exchange: bid lower, restructure as a long hold, or walk away.

**Expert: Make the building give you the tier** (Carianne Wells)
- About **110 sqm of existing footprint (ground and first floors)** is the guide for a clean seven-bed with en-suites.
- Permitted development (PD) limits: internal changes need no planning; a terrace can extend **3 m** without permission, or **6 m (terrace) / 8 m (detached)** under prior approval (a notification with **21 days** for neighbours to respond).
- The PD loft limit of about **40 cubic metres** measures the **dormer volume**, not the floor space.
- **Floor drops**: lower the ceilings to the licensing minimum (**often about 2.3 m**) and reclaim the height in the loft.
- **Sequence the works**: do the PD works while the house is still C3, then apply for the change of use, so the officer rules only on the use. This is done at risk.
- Showstoppers: a corner plot's side being treated as the "principal elevation"; Article 4, which removes the C3 to C4 PD route. Article 4 also works as a moat that protects existing stock.

**Expert: Building HMOs in 2026** (an experienced developer)
- The squeeze: a terrace that cost £300k to £330k all-in and refinanced at about £400k now costs £400k+ all-in, while refinance values are still in the low £400s. "The squeeze is arithmetic, not policy."
- **Build-Cost Rule**: appraise at today's costs and values. Treat capital that has not come back out within 2 to 3 years as left in.
- **Specification Rule**: design fire, acoustics and ventilation in at drawing stage (a fire panel costs about £2k built in, £10k to £12k retrofitted).
- **Operating Rule**: budget cleaning, redecoration at each turnover (about £150 a room) and maintenance as fixed costs.

**Expert: The best non-Article 4 area?** (Richard Nicholls)
- **C3 to C4 PD covers up to six people**. Seven or more is sui generis and needs planning everywhere.
- "HMOs are there to solve a local housing need, not to provide a strategy to investors." Ask about Article 4 fourth or fifth; ask about demand, tenant profile and your team first.

**Expert: Money left in isn't money lost** (case study)
- A six-bed the owner hoped would value at £350k to £400k valued at £290k to £300k. The blended roll was £39,000 (an average of £540 a room) against the £650 modelled. Yield 12 to 13%, checked at £50k to £60k a room.
- "The value didn't fall. The cost rose." Operating costs ran at **30 to 40% of gross** with full management.
- Five disciplines: sensible leverage; rents anchored to the **affordability floor** (the level, from ONS earnings data, below which rents in a well-run house should not fall); control of voids and maintenance; 2 to 4% rent growth to absorb yield drift; possible future Article 4 protection.
- **HMO X Rule**: underwrite the blended rent across the full fixed term, not the launch peak.

**Expert: The lending market** (George Sanford, Vibe Finance; also in the Buyer lens; figures illustrative)
- **"Price the whole loan, not the monthly"**: a rate near 3% can carry a 7% fee. Standard fees run from nil to about 2%.
- **Mortgage prisoner**: someone borrowing more than the open market will refinance, left with high-leverage lenders or product transfers.
- Valuers deduct **10 to 15%, sometimes 20%**, for bills, maintenance and voids before applying the multiplier.
- **"A lease lifts cash flow, not value"**: supported housing is valued at the market room rate.

**Expert: Fire safety** (Alice Ibbotson and a fire engineer)
- A **fire strategy** covers design and construction; a **fire risk assessment (FRA)** covers an occupied building.
- From about six beds, Approved Document B **Volume 2** can apply (lobbies, automatic vents), and lobbies can take rooms below size. Bring in a fire engineer at drawing stage; late fixes have cost £20k to £30k.
- **Grade A** alarms: tested weekly, serviced **every six months** (BS 5839). **Grade D**: tested monthly. Emergency lighting: tested monthly, serviced yearly. Do a logged monthly walk-round.
- A competent assessor should do a full FRA about **every four years, with a written review each year**. A national register is expected from about **2029**.
- Lets by the room are treated as bedsit-type (a higher fire standard) compared with a joint-tenancy shared house. No e-bike or e-scooter charging in bedrooms or escape routes.

## 2. Investor & Buyer lens
**Purpose**: the "valuation-clarity lens" for buying an established HMO on its income. **Rule**: "Buy the tier it holds, not the price it asks." Buyers who complete with HMO X get 12 months of Hub support from exchange.

**Framework**
- "The asking price is a claim about tier", usually a generous one.
- **Working valuation** = the yield band applied to home tier rent. **Asking-versus-working gap** = "either your negotiating room or your overpayment".
- An old asking price that never sold is evidence of where the market refused to buy, not a reference point.
- Compliance is now part of the price: "Read before the offer, it is leverage. Found after completion, it is cost."

**Diagnostic: three checks**
1. **What am I inheriting?** The licence (it does not transfer), lawful use, FRA (a standalone document), alarm (BS 5839), emergency lighting (BS 5266), CP12 (within 12 months), EICR (within 5 years), EPC (E or above), Renters' Rights tenancies and the title.
2. **What will the lender say?** Does it stack at 100% of home tier, or only at 115%?
3. **Can I act?** Have you spoken to a broker, do you know your borrowing and deposit, have you tested the numbers at home tier, and can you move fast?
- "A surveyor who arrives with the rent roll... values the going concern. One who arrives cold values a house."

**Worked Scheme** (a seven-bed sui generis HMO bought through Committed Sale)
- Rooms averaged £646 against a Tier 3 home tier of about £652, so about **99%**. With three of seven rooms en-suite, it was named **Tier 3, not Tier 2**.
- Yield band 11.00 to 11.50%. The agreed price was at about 11.08%, the keen end, earned by full compliance and secured lawful use.
- **One building, four numbers**: about £450k as a house; about £483k clearing (a buyer's net view at about 42.5% costs); £490k agreed; about £540k on a refinance basis.

**Expert: Make the lender value the business** (George Sanford)
- **Bricks-and-mortar** valuation is the default on most small C4 HMOs. **Investment** (income) valuation is the default on sui generis and possible on well-specified C4.
- About **75% LTV / 25% deposit** is typical. A bigger deposit is rarely the right fix. Settle finance before the offer.

**Expert: The opportunity in each tier** (Richard Nicholls)
- "A purchase is a forecast." Read the tier on amenity, size and condition.
- **Test 1 (HMO X Rule)**: an HMO must out-invest a local buy-to-let on a net basis, or don't buy it.
- **Test 2**: justify every pound above the bricks value with evidenced income and a reason that is hard to copy.
- **Thirty-second check**: gross income ÷ rooms ÷ 12. About £450 where rooms should make £600 suggests Tier 4 or 5.
- Tiers 1 and 2 are valued on income; Tiers 4 and 5 on bricks and mortar. Ask for legionella, asbestos and buildings insurance documents up front.

**Expert: Corner shop, Tesco Express, supermarket** (Jon Rose)
- Corner shop: 1 to 5 HMOs, self-managed. Tesco Express: part-time help from about 6. Supermarket: systemised at 10 to 12.
- Rules: "Maintenance is not a cost line, it is tier protection." "Set your buying criteria once, then buy to it." "Knowing your own skill set is the real decision." In 2026, "eight well-run HMOs is a supermarket".

## 3. Exit & Seller lens
**Purpose**: the "realisation lens". **Rule**: "Sell the tier it holds, on the timeline you chose." "Plan the exit; don't trigger it."

**Framework**
- Price from the HMO's evidence, not from what you need. Otherwise you get "a price chased downward through two quarters of reductions".
- Know the **asking-versus-clearing gap** as a number. Hold the tier until completion. Every loose end is discounted twice: once for the cost of fixing it and once for the doubt it creates.
- Plan the exit **12 to 24 months** ahead.

**Diagnostic: five questions**
1. Am I pricing to today's percentage? (Sustainable roll ÷ clearing yield.)
2. Do I know the gap as a number?
3. Is the pack clean? "The pack is not paperwork. It is price."
4. Have I chosen the channel on purpose? **95 to 110% of home tier → Committed Sale**; auction for speed and certainty; off-market for discretion; or hold and review.
5. Will I hold the tier until completion?

**Worked Scheme** (a seven-bed with its sui generis use secured by an LDC)
- About **99% of Tier 3**. Four numbers: about £450k as a house (a floor, not an exit); about £483k clearing as-is; about £510k optimised; about £540k on a refinance basis.
- The previous £500k+ asking was withdrawn unsold: 3.4% above clearing, 10.6% below the finance figure.
- Lever: re-tier from Tier 3 to Tier 2 by adding en-suites (**£3,500 to £7,000 a room**), adding about £27k of value for £11k to £21k of work.
- Route: Committed Sale, either now (exchange about **56 days** from acceptance) or after **9 to 12 months** of improvement.

**Expert: Selling in 2026** (Richard Nicholls)
- "More than a valuation, more than a gross yield, more than some marketing."
- Committed Sale: a forfeitable deposit and **56-day exclusivity**. HMO X Auctions: the UK's first HMO auction house, launched in 2025, with **600,000+ registered buyers**. Off-market through the Deal Thread, plus bespoke sales.

**Expert: Lawful use** (Carianne Wells)
- An **LDC** (Section 191, Town and Country Planning Act 1990) takes **8 to 12 weeks**. Lenders now want the certificate itself.
- **Two routes**: use that began before the Article 4 Direction, or **10 years' continuous use with no enforcement action**.
- Rules: "Sit tight near the ten-year line." "Ten years fixes planning, not building regs."
- A proposed-use certificate is "largely not worth the paper". **Neighbour declarations** carry significant weight. **Care use is C2**, not C4.
- The "shady seven": a seven-person licence with no sui generis consent. One fix is to drop back to six, secure planning, then upgrade to seven.

# Batch D notes: Operator and Add Value lenses, Community Calls Ep. 1 to 17, RRA videos

Read in full. Guest figures are their view on the day, given as illustration, not fixed rates or settled policy.

## 1. Operator lens

**Purpose:** the "hold" (build to refinance/sale), where an HMO spends most of its life and risk. Spot quiet underperformance and close it cost-effectively, by self-managing to a professional standard or with a specialist agent (equally legitimate).
**Rule / house phrases:** "Operate to the tier it holds, not the rooms it fills." "Run the house on the second number." "Compliance drift is tier slippage by another name." "The agent runs the asset, and the owner runs the agent." "The right agent at 12% is cheaper than the wrong agent at 8%." "Being at tier is the goal, not a missed opportunity."

**Framework**
- Home tier percentage = current rent ÷ the sustainable rent the asset should command at its tier. Occupancy says the rooms are full; the percentage says whether the rent is right. Fully let at 83% = under-optimised, with no empty room to warn you.
- Slippage is the default: left passive, about one tier every 24 to 36 months (Tier 1 is Tier 2 by year three). It shows up as slower lets and tenants negotiating, not voids. Re-baseline every year.
- Capex reserve about £500 to £1,000 per room per year, plus a refit around year five. Skipping it "borrows from the refinance". Gold-plating past the tier is the opposite leak, because the affordability floor caps rent.
- Example six-bed: 115% at launch. Left passive, it is worth about £316,000 by year five (84%, at 12%); run with discipline, about £400,000.

**Diagnostic (ladder)**
- 115%+ launch premium (will fade); 100% home tier; 85 to 95% drift (recoverable, often cheaply); under 85% structural (re-tier or change strategy).
- Name the **one binding bottleneck**: room size, en-suites, communal quality, management, or rents left behind.
- The sharpest self-test question: "What percentage will my next refinance value at?"

**Worked Health Check:** seven-bed, 12% agent, £54,300/yr, about 99% of Tier 3: "evidenced relief". The bottleneck is structural (3 of 7 en-suite), so the fix is capex, not a rent review. En-suites move rooms from about £585–600 to £700–710; +£4,500/yr for £11,000 to £21,000 (£3,500 to £7,000 a room); at about 10% that is +£45,000 of value, a payback of 2 to 3.5 years. "Protect the value before you add to it": licence, gas, EICR, 12 months of accounts, FRA and logs, PAT, insurance, Legionella, measured plan. A thin pack widens the yield a valuer applies. Evidence rent from actual tenancies.

**Management (Holly Hunt, HMO X)**
- On one six-bed, a specialist agent charging about two points more than an 8% high-street agent made the owner over £1,000 a month more.
- Disciplines: market before the room is empty, reply within the hour; about 7-day turnarounds with a pre-checkout inspection; small regular rent rises (£15 a year beats a catch-up after 3 years); a standing contractor network with no mark-ups.
- Self-manage only with all four: time, temperament, local knowledge, standards. Penalty notices go to the licence holder, not the agent.

**Insurance (a specialist broker): "Insured is not covered"**
- About 93% of landlords have the rebuild value wrong; about 7 in 10 of those are underinsured. **Averaging:** insured for half, every claim is cut by half (£20,000 claim pays £10,000).
- **Reinstatement Rule:** insure the rebuild cost, never market value (a converted HMO can cost about 2.5× its value to rebuild). Use a calculator, or a RICS desktop report for anything unusual. Re-check at least every 3 years.
- **Disclosure Rule:** declare it as an HMO plus every material fact: construction, roof, cladding, movement or flooding, cooking in rooms (a microwave counts), CCJs. An omission can void even an unrelated claim.
- Tenant type is the biggest cause of voided HMO claims. If a provider or rent-to-rent operator places the occupants, declare it as **sublet** (plus social housing or supported living if relevant), even with one occupant.
- **Cover Rule:** fix the cover first (including malicious damage by tenant), then the price. The right cover is usually only tens of pounds a year more. Review every year.

**HHSRS (Alice Ibbotson with a landlord-defence specialist)**
- Housing Act 2004 tool for all rented homes. **Category 1 = score above 1,000**: the council must act. Test: high probability within 12 months of death, serious injury or a doctor or hospital visit.
- **2026 amendment in force 23 June 2026**, with little press. Hazards cut from 29 to 21; adds baseline indicators and worked case studies. "It got sharper", not gentler.
- Scored against the **most vulnerable age group** who could live there within 12 months, across all seasons. No heating or hot water is Category 1 in any month. (An FRA uses the actual occupants.)
- A licence and the HHSRS are different tests.
- Studio of 13 sqm = Category 1; about **18 sqm** needed.
- Detection: "an alarm per storey" is a floor. Most HMOs: **Grade D1, Category LD1** (mains, interlinked), plus bedroom sounders.
- Shower tray step over **150mm** needs a handrail or intermediate step. Heating: at −1°C outside, living room 21°C and other rooms 18°C; hot water stored at 60°C, delivered up to 45°C.
- Enforcement is now fine-led: a fire-door gap over 4mm fails. About £7,000 for one Category 1 hazard; about £17,000 start for an unlicensed HMO plus about £12,000 per management breach; one case over £225,000. Councils often use the strict-liability **HMO Management Regulations**, where "I was getting it fixed" is no defence.
- Buying: liability transfers on completion. Combined HHSRS + FRA "property MOT" about £1,000 to £1,500, every 5 years or so.

**Fire safety (Alice Ibbotson with a former senior fire officer)**
- **Fire strategy** = design stage (building regs). **FRA** = occupied building, covering both the precautions and the people.
- Approved Document B: B1 escape, B3 internal spread, B5 fire-service access. At about 6 to 7+ beds **ADB Volume 2** may apply (lobbies, AOVs), and lobbies can break room sizes; the cut-off varies by council.
- A late strategy demand from building control costs £20,000 to £30,000. Bring the engineer in at drawing stage; use one detection contractor; keep the Regulation 38 data; do a pre-occupation FRA.
- **Grade A** panel: test weekly, service six-monthly (BS 5839). **Grade D**: test monthly, service annually. **Emergency lighting**: test monthly, service annually. Keep a logbook recording the fix, not just the fault; do a monthly logged walk round.
- **Competent assessor:** full FRA roughly every **4 years** with a written annual review; self-assessing risks bias. A national register is expected about **2029**.
- LACORS: a joint-tenancy shared house is lower risk than individual room lets (bedsit-type). The RRA's push to room tenancies can raise the standard.
- No e-bike or e-scooter charging in bedrooms or escape routes. A change of tenant type changes the FRA, the insurance and the lending.
- Often missing: smoke seals, fake or hollow-core fire doors, inner rooms, bedroom detection. Over half of HMO X onboardings lack an adequate FRA.

**EPC / MEES (an energy assessor)**
- Most assessors carry no PI insurance, so cannot advise. Evidence work as it happens: bring the assessor in **before** the refurb.
- Today's EPC is cost-based (gas flatters it). RdSAP was revised June 2026.
- **Home Energy Model:** three carbon measures (fabric, heating, smart readiness). Expected MEES test: C on fabric (or exemption) **plus** C on heating or smart readiness. **Not confirmed.** A current gas Band C may be grandfathered to about 2036/37: review a "hovering C" now.
- Quick wins: smart TRV zone control (1 to 4 points), LED lighting, evidenced roof insulation, double glazing, working shutters. Insulate before any heat pump.

**RRA on the ground (agents, Ep. 4 and 17)**
- Quiet start: tenant guides unread, tenants still ask for fixed terms. Vetting moves earlier (how long they plan to stay, fit with the house, never discrimination). Fund retention. Keep one retrievable record system, enough for a possession bundle. "The Act did not change the job; it raised the cost of doing it badly."
- Six months on: tighter referencing means some tenants cannot move; agents have lost renewal fees. Keep rents at market: under-renting hurts both the tenant and the sale.
- Mid-market rooms let best; premium rooms suit perhaps 5 to 10% of local demand. HMO X splits "professional" from "working" tenants.

**Crowded markets (south-coast agent, Ep. 13)**
- The problem is choice, not price: 500 to 600 rooms listed; new adverts drop off page one within hours. Reply within about 10 minutes with a video tour; view until 8pm and at weekends.
- Test your agent: search for your room as a tenant would. Page 4 or 5 is your answer.
- Agent fees: good HMO agents charge about 10 to 15% plus VAT; question anything under 10%. Read the exit terms.

**Rent-to-rent rescue (Ep. 16)**
- The landlord got £8,000 a month from a roll of about £36,000. Licences in the operator's name meant 17 new applications. Deposits had gone (about £8,000 re-protected): check with the scheme, not the certificate. Serve change-of-landlord and change-of-management notices.
- Improve within the tier with tenants in situ: Tier 4 lifted to the top of Tier 3, reaching about £57,000 a month after 18 months.
- New conversions let to professionals for 2 to 3 years, then settle into "working" tenants. A mixed en-suite and shared portfolio ran at about 98%. Under Section 8, the compliance paper trail is your case.

**Case study:** being at the top of Tier 3 when fully let is a strong position; the en-suite is an optional, costed route to Tier 2. Slippage is sticky. "Think like the CEO of your property business."

## 1b. Add Value lens

**Purpose:** buy Tier 4/5 stock that has slipped. The inherited position (lawful use, grandfather rights, compliance) is the base value driver. **Rule:** "Spec to the tier it holds, not past it." "Lender surveyors fund rent, not invoices." "Your entry price is your worst comparable."
- Ignore what you paid: diagnose the percentage from inspection, rent roll and management. Separate management gaps (an operator's play, e.g. 82% to 95%) from renovation gaps.
- **Genuine value-add:** extensions; reconfiguring to add bedrooms; all en-suite; planning class change; loft or floor drop; compliance work that unlocks lending. Paint and better management pay through the margin, not the valuation.
- **Tier 1 needs all four:** rooms 14 to 16 sqm single / 18 to 22 sqm double; A1 location; hotel-grade acoustics (£1,500 to £3,000 a room); real work-from-home space.
- **Three checks:** (1) what you inherit: lawful use (consent or LDC), Article 4, grandfather rights, licence, FRA, alarm (BS 5839), lighting (BS 5266), CP12 within 12 months, EICR within 5 years, RRA tenancies, title. Each gap is a lever before the offer and your cost after it. (2) An honest target tier. (3) Will a surveyor fund it? Yields are typically 11.5 to 12.5% in the Midlands and north.
- **Refinance trap:** a surveyor uses your own purchase as the comparable unless there is material change. Three routes count: shared to en-suite, C4 to sui generis, added floor space. Cosmetics and a change of tenant type alone do not. Get the LDC even if a statutory declaration was enough to complete.
- **Craft:** internal reconfiguration needs no planning. Terrace rear extension: 3m under PD, up to 6m by prior approval (21 days). The roughly 40 cubic metre loft limit measures the dormer, not the floor. A floor drop (ceilings down to about 2.3m) gains loft height. PD works first, change of use last. Close compliance before marketing (about £8k to £12k): surveyors price every open item twice.
- Worked scheme (five-bed northern terrace at 74%): the capex did not pay back on day one; it only stacked after an 18-month hold or at a keener entry price. The lesson is that the entry price decides. Its figures contain errors (see section 3).

## 4. Quiz candidates

1. Home tier percentage measures? **Current rent as a share of the sustainable rent at its tier.** Wrong: rooms let; gross yield; rent v town average. Why: occupancy hides soft rents.
2. Fully let at 83%? **Under-optimised (drift).** Wrong: performing well; at tier; launch premium. Why: soft rent clears fast and looks like demand.
3. How fast does a passive HMO slip? **One tier every 24 to 36 months.** Wrong: every 6 months; every 10 years; never. Why: slippage is the default.
4. Annual capex reserve? **About £500 to £1,000 per room.** Wrong: £50; £5,000; nil until year 5. Why: plus a refit around year five.
5. Insured at half the rebuild cost, a £20,000 claim pays? **£10,000.** Wrong: £20,000; nothing; £15,000. Why: averaging.
6. Basis for the buildings sum insured? **Rebuild cost, re-checked every 3 years.** Wrong: market value; price paid; loan balance. Why: rebuild can far exceed value.
7. A provider places the occupants. Declare as? **Sublet.** Wrong: standard AST; nothing; "DSS". Why: tenant type is the top cause of voided claims.
8. Category 1 HHSRS score? **Above 1,000.** Wrong: 100; 50; any hazard. Why: the council must act.
9. 2026 HHSRS amendment date? **23 June 2026.** Wrong: 1 May 2026; 31 May 2026; April 2027. Why: 29 hazards cut to 21.
10. HHSRS assesses hazards against? **The most vulnerable group who could live there within 12 months.** Wrong: current tenants; the landlord; an average adult. Why: an FRA uses the actual occupants.
11. Studio size needed under the new baseline? **About 18 sqm.** Wrong: 6.51; 10.22; 13. Why: 13 sqm is the worked Category 1 example.
12. Detection for most HMOs? **Grade D1, Category LD1.** Wrong: one alarm per storey; Grade F; kitchen heat detector only. Why: one per storey is a floor.
13. Grade A panel servicing? **Six-monthly (tested weekly).** Wrong: 5-yearly; monthly; on tenant change. Why: BS 5839.
14. Full FRA frequency? **About every 4 years plus a written annual review.** Wrong: once; every 10 years; never if self-done. Why: needs a competent assessor.
15. Joint tenancy to room agreements? **Can reclassify as bedsit-type and raise the fire standard.** Wrong: lowers it; no effect; ends licensing. Why: LACORS.
16. Maximum fire-door gap? **4mm.** Wrong: 10mm; 25mm; 44mm. Why: 44mm is the door thickness.
17. Which works justify a commercial revaluation? **En-suite conversion, class change or added floor space.** Wrong: paint and furniture; student to professional; supported-living lease. Why: material change is needed.
18. Basic-rate property income tax from April 2027? **24%.** Wrong: 20%; 22%; 40%. Why: two 2% rises; company-held property unaffected.
